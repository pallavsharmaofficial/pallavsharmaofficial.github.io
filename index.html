KundliSaar Flutter · Dart

A Vedic astrology app with its own ephemeris. Rather than licence Swiss Ephemeris — which would have forced the whole project open under AGPL — I fitted a Poisson series to JPL's DE440s and emitted it as Dart tables. Planetary longitudes land within about an arcsecond from 1900 to 2070, computed on the handset with the radio off.

On top of that: sixteen divisional charts, Vimshottari to three levels with dated periods, a full panchang checked against published almanacs to the minute, Tajika varshphal, ashtakavarga, shadbala, and a predictive layer that names the chart factor behind every sentence it writes.

  • 450+ tests, verified against skyfield and DE440s
  • 7,084 Indian towns, searchable without diacritics
  • Hindi and English throughout; nothing leaves the device

Testing and QA, as a service on its own

Plenty of teams can build. Far fewer can tell you, with evidence, whether what they built actually works. I can do this for software I did not write.

A test plan first

Written down before anything is run: what matters, what breaks the release, what we are deliberately not testing and why. You approve it, so nobody argues about scope afterwards.

Automation where it pays

Suites that run on every push and block a bad merge: unit, widget and integration, API contracts, and golden tests for anything visual. Automation is for what runs a hundred times.

Manual where it is the right tool

Exploratory passes, real devices, bad networks, the cheap Android phone your actual users have. Regional language input, right-to-left text, accessibility. A script will not find what a person finds.

Reports you can act on

Not a wall of green ticks. What was covered, what was not, every defect with the exact steps to reproduce it, severity, and a plain recommendation on whether to ship.

Release verification

The pass before a build goes out: smoke on the release candidate, upgrade path from the version already installed, store compliance, permissions and privacy declarations, rollback plan.

Setting it up to last

If you would rather own this yourself, I will set up the harness, write the first suites and the conventions, and hand it to your team working.

The honest version of this: automated tests catch regressions, not design mistakes, and a hundred percent coverage number tells you almost nothing. I will tell you what the testing does and does not buy you, rather than selling you a number.
